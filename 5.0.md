The active heat that must be rejected by the vehicle’s cooling system (battery, motors, inverters) is relatively small—typically only a few kilowatts under highway cruise. Thermoelectric generator (TEG) array on the residual low-grade stream
After the Stirling has extracted its work, the remaining heat still has a useful temperature gradient. Place high-ZT thermoelectric modules in the cascade path. They convert a further 5–8 % of that residual heat directly into electricity that is fed back onto the high-voltage DC bus. Every watt recovered electrically is a watt that never reaches the final radiator.
EL-50 predictive “zero-radiator / maximum-utilization” mode
The controller now treats the final radiator as a last-resort dump rather than the default sink. It continuously solves:Can the residual heat be returned to the intermediate or low-temperature PCM zones?
Can it supply cabin heating or battery thermal conditioning?
Can it drive an absorption or thermoelectric cooler for cabin air-conditioning (so the heat itself becomes the energy source for cooling instead of being rejected while the AC compressor runs)?
Only when all useful sinks are saturated does any heat go to the radiator.On routes where heating or battery conditioning is needed, or when solar input is high and the thermal reservoir has headroom, the system can operate for long periods with essentially zero net heat exhaust to the atmosphere.
